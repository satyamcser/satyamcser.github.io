About

Research identity

I am an AI researcher and engineer, currently an Integrated MS-PhD student at the KAIST Kim Jaechul Graduate School of AI in the Automotive Intelligence X Electrification (AXE) Lab. My research focuses on end-to-end planning for autonomous driving: what planning modules represent in their latent states, and the control architectures that turn those plans into motion.

The thread through my work is intelligence that holds up outside the lab: systems that are interpretable, stable, and safe when they meet the real world. That runs from self-driving cars to robots and physical AI, through reinforcement learning and safe control, to tools for understanding what large models have learned.

Alongside research, I build hands-on robotics systems and mentor students and engineers who want to go from theory to working robots across perception, planning, control, and simulation. I also serve the research community as a NeurIPS 2026 reviewer and an AAAI 2026 Program Committee member.

Research

Research directions

Four connected directions, all aimed at AI that perceives, decides, and acts reliably in the physical world.

01

Autonomous Driving and Planning

End-to-end planning, latent planning states, perception-to-control pipelines, and controller design for self-driving systems.

02

Reinforcement Learning and Safe Control

Safe and explainable RL, constrained decision-making, and symbolic oversight for policies that stay predictable on real systems.

03

Interpretable AI and Model Dynamics

Koopman operators, spectral methods, and entropy signals for understanding learned models, from planning latents to semantic drift and hallucination in LLMs.

04

Robotics and Physical AI

Robot perception, mobile robots, motion planning, and simulation for embodied systems that close the loop from sensing to control.

Robotics · Physical AI

Intelligence that moves

A self-driving car is one kind of robot. Beyond the car, I work on physical AI more broadly: robots that perceive their surroundings, are developed and tested in simulation, and act safely in the real world. It is the same perception, planning, and control loop as my driving research, applied to a wider family of machines.

R1

Mobile robots

From a Jetson Nano self-driving robot car with deep-learning collision avoidance and path following to complete perception, planning, and control stacks.

R2

Robot perception

Detection, measurement, pose estimation, and 3D point clouds that turn sensor data into geometry a robot can act on.

R3

Motion, simulation and learning

Koopman-based one-shot motion planning, simulation, and safe reinforcement learning to shape robot behavior before it reaches hardware.

R4

Hands-on robotics mentoring

Guiding students and engineers from theory to working robots through project-based work in perception, planning, control, and simulation.
