About

Research identity

I am an AI engineer and researcher with experience across academic research, applied AI development, and research-driven product engineering. At KAIST, my current research direction focuses on end-to-end planning for autonomous driving, latent states of planning modules, and control architectures for self-driving systems.

My broader research agenda is centered on building intelligent systems that are not only accurate, but also interpretable, stable, safe, and useful in real environments. This includes reinforcement learning, semantic dynamics in language models, agentic AI, computer vision, robotics, and AI systems for education, healthcare, cybersecurity, and mobility.

Autonomy stack

From sensors to steering

My PhD research at KAIST AXE Lab focuses on end-to-end planning for autonomous driving: how planners represent the world in their latent states, and how those representations connect to control architectures for self-driving systems.

  • L1Perceiving the scene from cameras and LiDAR
  • L2Anticipating how surrounding agents will move
  • L3Planning safe, interpretable trajectories
  • L4Turning plans into stable vehicle control

Robotics · Embodied AI

Intelligence that moves

A self-driving car is one kind of robot. I am interested in the wider family of machines that sense, plan and act in the physical world, and in learning methods that keep them safe and predictable while they do it.

R1

Mobile robots

Built a self-driving robot car on a Jetson Nano with deep-learning collision avoidance, path following, and image recognition.

R2

Robot perception

Detection, measurement, and pose understanding that turn camera input into geometry a robot can act on.

R3

Learning to act

Reinforcement learning with safety constraints and symbolic oversight for decision-making on embodied platforms.

R4

Closing the loop

Connecting perception, planning, and control into systems that behave reliably outside the lab.
